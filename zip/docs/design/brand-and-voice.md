# oobCREATIVE Brand and Voice

## Purpose

oobCREATIVE helps capable people turn complex ideas, scattered communication, and underused technology into clear communication and practical systems people can understand, trust, and act on.

The brand should demonstrate strategic judgment, emotional intelligence, creative range, technical capability, and practical implementation.

It should not present oobCREATIVE as a large agency, a generic freelancer, an automated content factory, or a broad management consultancy.

## Core audience condition

The audience is capable. Their personal effort is compensating for missing structure.

They may carry developed judgment, meaningful responsibility, and an expensive communication or execution gap.

The brand must never treat them as unintelligent, lazy, technologically behind, or personally responsible for structural friction.

## Core movement

Help people move from:

- Personal overload to shared capability
- Fragmented communication to usable structure
- Difficult-to-explain value to clear decision pathways
- Tool experimentation to responsible practice
- Stalled projects to visible, controlled progress
- Private possibility to a credible real-world test

Do this without sacrificing quality, trust, depth, humanity, mission, agency, or possibility.

## Voice

The voice is:

- Clear
- Human
- Emotionally perceptive
- Direct
- Calm
- Specific
- Curious
- Practical
- Collaborative
- Experienced without self-importance

The voice should sound like an experienced person who sees the real problem and can explain the next move without performance or pressure.

## Point of view

First person is appropriate when it establishes accountability, perspective, or working style.

Keep Rodney visible enough to create trust. Do not make every page autobiographical.

Prefer:

> I help capable people make complicated ideas easier to understand, use, and move forward.

Avoid inflated founder language or claims of visionary transformation.

## Archetype communication rule

The named personas are internal.

Public communication should express:

1. A recognizable working moment
2. Respectful validation
3. The value the person is protecting
4. A desired movement
5. A bounded choice
6. Concrete evidence of what changes

The audience should feel understood before being asked to select a service.

## Writing style

Use:

- Short, complete sentences
- Natural variation in sentence length
- Strong headings
- Clear transitions
- Specific examples
- Concrete outcomes
- Purposeful white space
- Direct explanations of boundaries and choices

Avoid:

- Long comma-heavy sentences
- Repeated sentence openings
- Dense, undifferentiated lists
- Buzzwords
- Generic agency language
- Fear-based urgency
- Empty inspiration
- Technology hype
- Vague transformation promises
- Excessive consulting jargon
- Artificially clever copy that delays understanding

## Emotional intelligence

Recognition must not become diagnosis.

Validation must not become pity.

Emotional language must not manipulate fear or shame.

The objective is:

> I recognize where I am. I understand the available paths. I can choose how I want to move forward.

## AI positioning

Present AI as:

- Selective leverage
- Human-directed support
- A workflow component
- Subject to review
- Connected to a real responsibility
- Useful when it reduces friction without reducing accountability

Do not present AI as:

- A replacement for people
- An automatic transformation
- A reason to create fear
- A standalone answer to a broken decision process
- A content-volume machine

## Calls to action

Calls to action should confirm a choice rather than begin undefined consulting.

Prefer:

- Start with the situation that feels familiar
- Review the path
- Confirm fit and boundaries
- Start with the smallest safe next step
- Discuss the project
- Bring me the complicated part

Avoid inflated or urgent conversion language.

## Content guardrails

Do not invent:

- Clients
- Testimonials
- Statistics
- Revenue
- Awards
- Certifications
- Partnerships
- Outcomes
- Project details
- Years of experience
- Team members

Do not publish confidential client information.

Do not imply that communication work alone guarantees business, fundraising, operational, or organizational outcomes.
