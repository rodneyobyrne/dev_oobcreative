# Core Audience Strategy

## Core audience condition

oobCREATIVE is best positioned for capable, thoughtful people whose responsibilities, ideas, or ambitions have outgrown the communication and operating systems supporting them.

Their problem is not a lack of intelligence, creativity, commitment, or technology. Their personal effort is compensating for missing structure.

## Stronger commercial qualification

Avoid defining the audience as “smart people who are behind on AI.” That is too broad and can sound corrective.

Use:

> People with developed judgment, meaningful responsibility, and an expensive communication or execution gap.

The gap should have a visible cost:

- Leadership attention
- Lost opportunities
- Slow decisions
- Repeated revisions
- Staff overload
- Weak audience response
- Inconsistent communication
- Underused expertise
- Fragmented brand experiences
- Personal exhaustion

## Shared emotional pattern

Each persona fears losing something important while changing:

- Frederick protects quality.
- Jonathan protects the mission and the people it serves.
- Brendan protects professional agency and communication quality.
- Devon protects intellectual depth.
- Eli protects responsibility, accuracy, and trust.
- Dave protects trust and decision safety.
- Doug protects possibility and his emerging identity.

oobCREATIVE helps them gain structure without abandoning what made the work meaningful.

## Public audience groups

The seven personas are primarily buying-state archetypes, not seven separate industries.

Recommended public audience groups:

- Founder-led organizations
- Nonprofits and mission-driven teams
- Communications and operational leaders
- Experts and emerging independent professionals

Dave is best treated as a project-recovery circumstance that can occur within any audience.

## Commercial priorities

### Highest-value potential

- Frederick, when the organization has revenue and staff
- Devon, when expertise and demand are already proven
- Eli, when AI work is tied to real communication or workflow outcomes
- Jonathan, when funding and authority are established

### Strong fit with qualification requirements

- Brendan, when executive sponsorship exists
- Dave, when the recovery work begins with a bounded first phase

### Best served through structured offers

- Doug
- Early-stage versions of Devon
- Underfunded versions of Jonathan

## Core transformation

> Turn valuable thinking into clear, usable communication and practical systems that reduce pressure and make progress easier—without sacrificing quality, trust, depth, or humanity.

## Working positioning direction

> oobCREATIVE helps capable leaders turn complex ideas, scattered communication, and underused technology into practical systems people can understand, trust, and act on.

# Emotional Marketing System

## Strategic objective

The buyer should not feel that oobCREATIVE is diagnosing them and deciding later what to sell.

They should feel:

> I recognize where I am. I understand the available paths. I can choose how I want to move forward.

## Core movement

> Persona → emotional state → desired movement → clear service path → chosen level of support

The persona informs strategy internally. The visitor experiences recognition, choice, safety, and momentum.

## Six-stage emotional pathway

### 1. Recognition

“I see my situation here.”

Use recognizable moments and working language before service terminology.

### 2. Validation

“This is not happening because I am incapable.”

Name the structural cause without making the buyer the problem.

### 3. Agency

“There is a path I can choose.”

Present bounded choices rather than an undefined consultation.

### 4. Safety

“I understand what this involves.”

Clarify:

- What will be produced
- What decisions the client will make
- How involved they need to be
- Timeline
- Boundaries
- Investment expectations
- What will be different at completion

### 5. Momentum

“I can experience progress before everything is solved.”

Each service should include an early visible win.

### 6. Capability

“I am stronger after working with oobCREATIVE.”

The client should leave with reusable assets, clearer thinking, greater confidence, and stronger systems—not only completed deliverables.

## Emotional temperatures

| Persona | Emotional temperature |
|---|---|
| Frederick | Controlled pressure |
| Jonathan | Heavy responsibility |
| Brendan | Contained irritation |
| Devon | Intellectual frustration |
| Eli | Alert caution |
| Dave | Guarded distrust |
| Doug | Hopeful hesitation |

## What each persona needs from oobCREATIVE

| Persona | Relational need |
|---|---|
| Frederick | A translator who can make his judgment transferable |
| Jonathan | A burden-sharing partner who respects what is at stake |
| Brendan | Outside validation that the chaos is structural, not personal |
| Devon | An intellectual mirror who can clarify without flattening |
| Eli | A grounded co-investigator who will reject bad uses of AI |
| Dave | A calm truth-teller who does not overpromise |
| Doug | Encouraging constraint that turns preparation into participation |

## Participation modes

### Guided

The client does more of the work with structured support, templates, feedback, and accountability.

### Co-Created

oobCREATIVE and the client develop the thinking and materials together.

### Full Implementation

oobCREATIVE leads strategy, creation, coordination, and implementation within clear boundaries.

These are participation choices, not necessarily the primary public products.

## Messaging guardrails

Use:

- Plain language
- Recognizable working moments
- Respect for the buyer’s judgment
- Concrete outcomes
- Permission to choose
- Clear scope and boundaries
- AI as selective leverage

Avoid:

- Fear-based urgency
- “You are behind” language
- Technology hype
- Treating overwhelm as incompetence
- Vague transformation claims
- Excessive consulting jargon
- Creating dependency

# Service and Product Architecture

## Strategic distinction

The public system should separate:

- What the visitor is experiencing
- What movement they want
- What oobCREATIVE may build
- How much support they want

Personas guide messaging internally. They should not automatically become seven public products.

## Emotional entry pathways

Use these statements for a Start Here or Find Your Path experience:

1. Everything still depends on me.
2. Our work is difficult to explain.
3. Communication feels chaotic.
4. People do not understand the value of what I do.
5. We need a practical, responsible way to use AI.
6. A project has stalled or gone wrong.
7. I am ready to turn an idea into something real.

Each pathway should lead to:

- A recognizable situation
- The value the person is protecting
- A desired movement
- A bounded service path
- Relevant examples
- A participation choice
- A clear next step

## Recommended public service families

### Message, Positioning and Identity

Possible work:

- Brand and mission messaging
- Positioning
- Audience clarification
- Service and program descriptions
- Website copy
- Calls to action
- Expert positioning
- Core narrative development

Primary personas: Jonathan, Devon, Doug, and some versions of Frederick.

### Website Strategy and Visual Refresh

Possible work:

- Website architecture
- Audience pathways
- Content hierarchy
- Navigation and decision flow
- Website copy
- Visual direction
- Design and implementation
- Search and AI-discovery foundations

Primary personas: Frederick, Jonathan, Devon, and Dave.

### Communication Systems and Practical AI

Possible work:

- Communication workflows
- Content intake and approval
- Reusable message and content libraries
- AI use-case selection
- Human-review standards
- CRM, email, scheduling, and tool configuration
- Knowledge systems
- Team implementation support

Primary personas: Brendan, Eli, and Frederick.

### Campaigns, Launches and Storytelling

Possible work:

- Campaign messaging
- Program or service launches
- Fundraising communications
- Human-centered storytelling
- Video, audio, photography, and visual direction
- Email and social rollout
- Distribution planning

Primary personas: Jonathan, Doug, and some versions of Dave.

### Ongoing Communications Support

Possible work:

- Strategy and prioritization
- Content planning and editing
- Website updates
- Campaign support
- Visual and media guidance
- Workflow refinement
- Vendor coordination
- Review before publication

Primary personas: Jonathan, Brendan, and Frederick.

## Existing offer labels as modules

- Message & Identity Clarity
- Website & Visual Refresh
- Out-of-Box Implementation Toolkit
- Campaign & Launch Kit
- Communications System & Support
- Storytelling & Media Impact Package

Treat these as working modules or named packages beneath a clearer public service architecture, not automatically as six equal top-level products.

## Working persona-specific pathways

| Persona | Internal concept | Public-facing need |
|---|---|---|
| Frederick | Founder Capacity System | Help my team move without everything coming through me |
| Jonathan | Mission Communication System | Help us explain the work clearly without losing its meaning |
| Brendan | Communications Workflow Reset | Help communication stop feeling chaotic |
| Devon | Expertise-to-Offer System | Help people understand the value of what I do |
| Eli | Responsible AI Implementation Sprint | Help us use AI where it genuinely improves the work |
| Dave | Project Recovery Pathway | Help me recover this project without repeating the same mistake |
| Doug | Launch Clarity Sprint | Help me turn this idea into something real enough to test |

These are product hypotheses. Do not launch all seven publicly without validating demand, scope, delivery capacity, proof, and pricing.

## Product design requirements

A credible service path should include:

1. A recognizable starting condition
2. A protected value
3. A defined movement
4. A bounded scope
5. Concrete outputs
6. Participation options
7. An early visible win
8. A measurable completion state
9. Qualification and disqualification signals
10. A next step that confirms a choice rather than beginning undefined consulting

## Anti-consulting guardrail

Avoid:

> Book a call, explain everything, and oobCREATIVE will decide what you need.

Prefer:

> Choose the situation that feels most familiar, review the path designed for it, and use a conversation to confirm fit and boundaries.

## Strong qualification signals

- The problem has a visible cost.
- The buyer has authority or a committed sponsor.
- The organization can make decisions.
- The buyer values quality and collaboration.
- The work fits communication, media, website, workflow, or practical AI capabilities.
- The buyer is willing to select priorities.
- A specific event, transition, failure, or growth condition creates urgency.

## Disqualification signals

- The buyer wants unlimited access or undefined support.
- The buyer expects AI to replace judgment or accountability.
- The buyer wants volume without clarity.
- Leadership will not participate in required decisions.
- The organization lacks budget but expects custom full-service execution.
- The requested outcome depends mainly on management consulting outside oobCREATIVE’s scope.
- The buyer wants guaranteed business results that communication work alone cannot ensure.

## Many-to-many rule

One persona can need several service families, and one service family can serve several personas.

The persona determines:

- Entry language
- Emotional validation
- Objections
- Proof
- Participation style

The service family determines:

- Scope
- Deliverables
- Delivery method
- Pricing
- Operational capacity

The site should support:

1. Emotional navigation for visitors who know something is wrong but cannot name the service.

- Founder-led organizations

## Product-page structure

1. Searchable service heading
2. Emotional recognition statement

# Atlas and LLM Implementation Guidance

## Purpose

Use this document to keep automated content, recommendations, page drafts, and client-pathway logic aligned with the oobCREATIVE audience and product strategy.

## Core brand logic

oobCREATIVE helps capable people move from personal overload, fragmented communication, and underused technology toward clear communication, practical systems, and shared capability.

The work should feel human, collaborative, grounded, and useful.

## Persona handling

### Internal use

Use the named personas to reason about:

- Emotional state
- Entry language
- Objections
- Buying triggers
- Protected value
- Service relevance
- Participation preference
- Proof needed

### Public use

Do not normally mention persona names.

Translate the persona into:

- A recognizable situation
- A respectful emotional truth
- A desired movement
- A concrete outcome
- A clear choice

Example:

Internal classification: Frederick.

Public language:

> Your team is capable, but too many decisions still return to you.

## Content sequence

When generating pathway, service, or campaign content:

1. Name the recognizable situation.
2. Validate the person without diminishing them.
3. Identify what they may be trying to protect.
4. Present a small number of relevant paths.
5. Clarify what each path changes.
6. Offer Guided, Co-Created, or Full Implementation participation when relevant.
7. State concrete outputs and boundaries.
8. End with a next step that confirms a choice.

## Choice architecture

Present no more than three primary choices at one decision point unless the interface is intentionally designed as a screening experience.

Do not ask visitors to diagnose whether they need messaging, websites, AI, media, or systems before they understand the problem.

Do not hide all clarity behind a consultation.

## Offer logic

Use these public service families as the default:

- Message, Positioning and Identity
- Website Strategy and Visual Refresh
- Communication Systems and Practical AI
- Campaigns, Launches and Storytelling
- Ongoing Communications Support

Treat these as possible modules or package labels:

- Message & Identity Clarity
- Website & Visual Refresh
- Out-of-Box Implementation Toolkit
- Campaign & Launch Kit
- Communications System & Support
- Storytelling & Media Impact

Do not invent a new product name unless the task explicitly calls for concept development.

## AI language

Present AI as:

- Selective leverage
- Practical support
- A workflow component
- Subject to human review
- Connected to an actual responsibility

Do not present AI as:

- A replacement for people
- An automatic transformation
- A reason to create fear
- The solution to a broken decision process
- A standalone product without a concrete use case

## Tone

Preferred:

- Clear
- Respectful
- Emotionally perceptive
- Practical
- Direct
- Collaborative
- Specific
- Calm

Avoid:

- Condescension
- Hype
- Artificial urgency
- Empty inspiration
- Excessive jargon
- Generic future-of-work language
- Implying the audience is unintelligent or behind

## Strategic guardrails

- Persona names are not SEO keywords.
- Emotional statements are entry pathways, not necessarily page titles.
- Service pages need descriptive headings and concrete outcomes.
- Dave is often a temporary project-recovery condition rather than a stable audience.
- Product pathways should stay within oobCREATIVE’s credible communication, media, website, systems, and practical AI scope.
- Do not drift into broad management consulting unless explicitly requested.
- Do not create seven public products simply because there are seven personas.
- Preserve client agency.

# Brendan — The Overextended Communications Lead

## One-sentence definition

A capable communications professional held responsible for outcomes while working inside unclear priorities, fragmented inputs, and weak approval systems.

## Stable identity

Communications director, marketing manager, development communications lead, or the person functioning as an entire communications department.

## Organizational context

Every department considers its request urgent. Leadership changes direction after work begins. Brendan understands the strategic problem but often lacks the authority to solve its organizational causes.

## Emotional temperature

**Contained irritation**

## A moment from their day

A message marked “quick request” arrives. Brendan already knows it will require missing information, several revisions, and half a day of work.

## Visible problems

- Constant urgent requests
- Repeated revisions
- No reliable intake or approval process
- Content is recreated instead of reused
- Leadership expects AI efficiency without defining standards
- Brendan is treated as an internal production desk

## Private pain points

- He feels underused strategically and overused tactically.
- He is irritated by preventable revisions.
- He worries that saying no will make him appear uncooperative.
- He resents being asked to compensate for leadership indecision.
- He fears AI will become another source of low-quality requests.

## Internal contradiction

He wants leadership to value his strategic thinking, but his reliability keeps rewarding the organization for using him tactically.

## What they are protecting

**Professional agency and communication quality**

Brendan wants communication treated as a managed organizational function rather than an endless stream of creative favors.

## What they tell other people

- Everything is a priority.
- I can produce the work, but nobody is making the decision.
- AI is not going to fix a broken approval process.

## What they admit privately

> The organization does not have a content problem. It has a decision problem, and I am being asked to compensate for it.

## Desired movement

Turn communication from reactive production into a structured, respected function.

## Desired feeling

**Control and legitimacy**

## Buying triggers

- Rising content volume
- New leadership expectations
- Repeated missed deadlines
- Team burnout
- AI adoption mandate
- Campaign or website project exposing process failures

## What they may have already tried

- Project-management tools
- Intake forms nobody follows
- Content calendars
- Templates
- Working longer hours
- Quietly taking over tasks to keep quality intact

## What they resist

- Productivity advice that ignores organizational causes
- More tools without governance
- Outsiders who treat him as the problem
- AI positioned as a substitute for strategy or review

## Language they use

- We need a real approval process.
- I cannot prioritize when everything is urgent.
- The issue is upstream.
- We keep starting from zero.

## Language to avoid

- Work smarter.
- Just automate it.
- You need better time management.
- Content at scale.

## Primary public pathway

> Help communication stop feeling chaotic.

## Relevant oobCREATIVE service families

- Communication Systems and Practical AI
- Ongoing Communications Support
- Message, Positioning and Identity

## Working offer experience

A bounded workflow-reset engagement that establishes intake, priorities, approvals, reusable content resources, and human-reviewed AI support.

## Emotional impact of the work

The work validates that the recurring chaos is structural rather than a personal productivity failure and gives Brendan authority through a shared system.

## Observable completion evidence

- Defined request and intake process
- Approval roles and timelines
- Priority rules
- Reusable message and content library
- Documented AI review standards
- Fewer preventable revisions

## Likely objections

- Leadership will not follow the process.
- I do not control the people making requests.
- We already tried a form.
- I need executive support before this can work.

## Decision authority and budget considerations

Brendan may identify the problem but not control the budget. Strong fit usually requires an executive sponsor who will support process changes.

## Qualification signals

- Executive sponsorship
- Access to decision-makers
- High recurring cost from communication chaos
- Willingness to establish governance
- A team that will use shared resources

## Disqualification signals

- Expected to fix the system without leadership participation
- Organization wants only faster production
- No authority to change intake or approvals
- AI is being used mainly to reduce headcount

## Relationship to AI

Pragmatic and skeptical. He sees value in adaptation, outlining, repurposing, and preparation, but knows AI cannot repair broken priorities or accountability.

## Relationship to control

Moderate. He wants clear standards and decision rights more than personal control over every detail.

## Relationship to visibility

Often visible internally but not publicly. He wants recognition as a strategist, not personal brand visibility.

## Visual-generation notes

These notes describe emotional atmosphere rather than a fixed demographic identity.

- Interruptions, layered requests, open tabs, calendars, or marked-up materials
- Focused posture under contained pressure
- Avoid chaotic comedy or exaggerated burnout
- Show competence constrained by system friction

## Representative quote

> I do not need another tool. I need the organization to decide how communication is supposed to work.

# Dave — The Rebuilding Leader

## One-sentence definition

A leader responsible for recovering a failed, stalled, or disappointing project without repeating the same mistake.

## Stable identity

Founder, executive, program leader, marketing lead, or project owner who has inherited or survived a failed website, campaign, brand, media, or technology initiative.

## Organizational context

Money and time have already been spent. Existing assets are incomplete or difficult to use. Stakeholders disagree about what failed, and confidence in vendors or internal decisions has declined.

## Emotional temperature

**Guarded distrust**

## A moment from their day

A new vendor says the project needs a fresh start. Dave immediately thinks about the money already spent and wonders whether he is being sold the same mistake again.

## Visible problems

- Stalled project
- Incomplete or inconsistent deliverables
- Stakeholder disagreement
- Pressure to fix the problem quickly
- Uncertainty about salvage versus rebuild
- Low trust in proposals

## Private pain points

- He is angry about wasted resources.
- He is embarrassed that the problem remains unresolved.
- He is suspicious of polished promises.
- He worries about defending another expense.
- He no longer fully trusts his own judgment.

## Internal contradiction

He wants decisive progress, but the previous failure makes every new decision feel dangerous.

## What they are protecting

**Trust**

Dave is protecting the organization from another opaque commitment and protecting himself from being responsible for another bad decision.

## What they tell other people

- How do we know?
- What happens before we commit?
- I have heard that before.

## What they admit privately

> I cannot afford to get this wrong again.

## Desired movement

Turn uncertainty into a sequence of controlled, visible decisions.

## Desired feeling

**Safety and control**

## Buying triggers

- Missed deadline
- Vendor breakdown
- Leadership scrutiny
- Website or campaign failure
- Change in project ownership
- Need to recover before a public launch

## What they may have already tried

- Asking the original vendor for revisions
- Starting over informally
- Gathering more opinions
- Delaying a decision
- Adding scope in hopes of fixing the original problem

## What they resist

- Large opaque proposals
- Trust-the-process language
- Unnecessary rebuilding
- Pressure to ignore sunk costs without explanation
- Overconfidence

## Language they use

- What can be salvaged?
- Why did this fail?
- What is the smallest safe next step?
- Show me what changes before we move on.

## Language to avoid

- We need to start from scratch.
- This will be easy.
- You just chose the wrong vendor.
- Do not worry about the details.

## Primary public pathway

> Help me recover this project without repeating the same mistake.

## Relevant oobCREATIVE service families

- Website Strategy and Visual Refresh
- Communication Systems and Practical AI
- Campaigns, Launches and Storytelling

## Working offer experience

A project-recovery pathway beginning with a bounded review of existing assets, a salvage-versus-rebuild decision, and a phased repair plan.

## Emotional impact of the work

The work restores Dave’s ability to trust his own judgment by making uncertainty, tradeoffs, and progress visible.

## Observable completion evidence

- Clear explanation of failure points
- Documented salvage and rebuild decisions
- Phased scope with checkpoints
- Recovered usable assets
- Completion of the highest-priority repair

## Likely objections

- I have already paid for this once.
- How is your process different?
- What prevents scope from expanding?
- Can we keep any of the existing work?

## Decision authority and budget considerations

Dave may have strong urgency and budget access, but the project often carries politics, blame, and unstable scope. A tightly bounded first phase is required.

## Qualification signals

- Access to existing files and decisions
- Authority to choose a recovery path
- Willingness to separate sunk cost from usable value
- Acceptance of phased work and review checkpoints

## Disqualification signals

- Wants guaranteed recovery without reviewing the cause
- Cannot provide access to prior work
- Internal stakeholders refuse decisions
- Expects oobCREATIVE to absorb prior vendor responsibility

## Relationship to AI

Relevant only when the failed project involves tools, content workflows, or automation. AI should not be added as a novelty during recovery.

## Relationship to control

High. Dave needs explicit checkpoints, options, and documented decisions.
