# Building the Backline

## Creating the communications and fundraising structure behind Rock and Roll Academy Aspen's growth

Rock and Roll Academy Aspen had already built a youth program students returned to, families valued, and communities supported.

Students formed real bands, made creative decisions, performed publicly, returned as mentors, and carried the experience into education and professional music.

The challenge was not proving that the program worked.

The challenge was building the communication, fundraising, and organizational structure required to sustain and expand it.

## The situation

The Academy's story was spread across grant applications, event flyers, staff knowledge, individual relationships, and years of student experience.

Different audiences needed different doorways:

- Parents needed to understand the program.
- Students needed to see themselves in a band.
- Funders needed consistent facts and defensible impact.
- Sponsors needed to understand what their support made possible.
- Community members needed a reason to attend.
- Major donors needed to understand why a successful program still required infrastructure.

Expansion into Glenwood Springs also created a larger regional identity and a more urgent need for leadership, fundraising, public relations, program facilitation, and communication capacity.

## Strategic response

oobCREATIVE approached the work as a connected communications system rather than a series of unrelated promotional requests.

The central message clarified that Rock and Roll Academy is more than music lessons. It is a student-led environment where young people choose, collaborate, develop independence, and experience the responsibility of public performance.

Working language included:

> Way more than just music lessons.

> Come as you are. Leave in a band.

> Your gift supports the structure behind the stage.

## Work completed

### Program and regional positioning

- Clarified student choice, independence, belonging, collaboration, public performance, and long-term pathways
- Established Rock and Roll Academy Aspen as the legal nonprofit
- Clarified Aspen and Glenwood Springs as two program locations
- Developed a broader Roaring Fork regional story without implying a separate nonprofit

### Grant and organizational knowledge system

- Reviewed prior grant applications
- Organized recurring questions and answers
- Standardized program descriptions, numbers, names, and impact claims
- Created short and long answer options
- Built partnership and story banks
- Developed city-specific tailoring guidance
- Flagged unsupported or inconsistent claims

Funding work included support for community-foundation, municipal arts, Glenwood Springs, NAMM Foundation, and other opportunity research.

### Sustainability campaign

The campaign separated two financial needs:

- $130,000 immediate sustainability campaign
- $200,000 ongoing annual operating need

Work included:

- Campaign narrative
- Donor-audience strategy
- Landing page
- Donation and sponsorship pathways
- QR-code access
- Donor handouts
- Campaign flyers
- Major-donor and sponsor messaging
- Launch sequence
- Newsletter and press planning
- Donor acknowledgment recommendations

### Performances as growth infrastructure

Concerts were treated as opportunities to:

- Celebrate students
- Demonstrate impact
- Recruit families
- Recognize sponsors
- Build community awareness
- Generate donations
- Collect photographs and stories
- Strengthen media relationships

Promotion supported showcases, recurring concerts, summer programs, pop-up bands, Mountain Fair, Glenwood Springs programming, and other public performances.

### Sponsorship and storytelling

- Revised sponsorship positioning around demonstrated impact
- Developed donor and sponsor packages
- Supported customized sponsor communication and fulfillment
- Created student, parent, and alumni interview systems
- Identified alumni education, mentorship, and career stories

### Internal capacity

- Supported board scheduling and virtual access
- Shared meeting notes and transcripts
- Helped organize strategic and nonprofit records
- Identified campaign tasks and dependencies
- Separated immediate fundraising from long-term sustainability

## Verified outcomes

- Unified Aspen–Glenwood Springs regional story
- Published sustainability campaign landing page
- Complete $130,000 campaign message and visual system
- Reusable grant answer and organizational data library
- Multiple completed grant applications and funding-fit analyses
- Regional press-release distribution
- Coordinated promotion of nine bands and nearly 50 young musicians
- $1,144.85 in documented donations around a May showcase, with shared attribution
- $1,500 Alpine Bank sponsorship renewal supported through updated outreach and materials
- Materials adopted and shared by board members and partners

## Not yet proven

- Completion of the $130,000 campaign
- Year-over-year donation growth
- Enrollment increases attributable to communication
- Website-conversion improvements
- Earned-media placement totals
- Long-term sponsorship growth
- Administrative time saved
- Achievement of the $200,000 annual goal

## Larger result

Rock and Roll Academy now has a clearer way to explain both sides of its story:

> The students create the music.  
> The community helps build the structure that keeps the stage available to them.
