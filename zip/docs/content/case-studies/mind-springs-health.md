# Creative Capacity Across a Community Mental Health System

## Supporting brand, staff communication, recruitment, public response, and community outreach for Mind Springs Health and West Springs Hospital

Mind Springs Health and West Springs Hospital served communities across a large rural and mountain region of Colorado.

The communication environment was unusually demanding. Internal staff, patients, families, job candidates, local communities, media organizations, and public stakeholders each needed different information—often under time pressure and within the reputational and human stakes of community mental health.

Rodney O'Byrne supported the organization as Marketing and Brand Strategist from November 2020 through June 2024.

## The challenge

The work required more than producing individual graphics.

A multi-brand behavioral-health system needed creative and communication support across:

- Internal staff communication
- Recruitment and job promotion
- Public-information response
- Mental-health education
- Community outreach
- Advertising and campaigns
- Social media
- Brand consistency

The public portfolio description identifies strategic mental-health education campaigns and creative communication planning that supported board and executive leadership, more than 500 staff members, and 14 Mind Springs Health and West Springs Hospital communities in Northwest Colorado.

## Role and contribution

### Brand and creative direction

- Supported the strategic design evolution of three Mind Springs organizational brand channels
- Maintained visual consistency across internal and external materials
- Translated complex behavioral-health topics into audience-ready communication

### Internal communication

- Produced and designed internal communication for more than 500 employees
- Supported leadership messages, organizational updates, education, and staff-facing materials
- Helped make information usable across multiple locations and roles

### Recruitment communication

- Collaborated with HR and recruiting teams
- Developed focused messaging and job-promotion techniques
- Created visual and written materials supporting workforce needs

### Public-information and media-response support

- Worked closely with the Vice President of Communications and Public Information Officer
- Supported strategic responses involving news publications and public attention
- Produced communication materials within a high-stakes behavioral-health environment

### Campaigns and outreach

- Designed promotions and advertising campaigns supporting organizational goals
- Developed community-outreach communication
- Supported cross-agency education
- Created and managed social-media content

## What this work demonstrates

### Communication across multiple audiences

The role required one organization to communicate differently with employees, prospective staff, patients, families, communities, media, and partners without losing consistency.

### Strategy connected to daily production

The work moved continuously between leadership priorities and the practical materials required to put those priorities into use.

### Behavioral-health judgment

Mental-health communication requires accuracy, dignity, accessibility, and restraint. The work needed to inform and motivate without sensationalizing the people or circumstances involved.

### Capacity under pressure

Serving as the sole graphic designer and media producer for an organization of more than 500 employees required prioritization, rapid interpretation, reusable standards, and the ability to move among many communication formats.
