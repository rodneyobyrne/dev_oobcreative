# Frederick — The Bottlenecked Founder

## One-sentence definition

An established founder whose judgment built the organization but has become the point through which too many decisions must pass.

## Stable identity

Founder, owner, or principal of an established small organization with staff, clients, and a reputation worth protecting.

## Organizational context

The business grew around Frederick’s instincts, standards, and relationships. Services evolved and staff were added, but the organization never fully translated his judgment into shared systems.

## Emotional temperature

**Controlled pressure**

## A moment from their day

A document arrives for approval late in the evening. Frederick changes nearly everything, sends it back, and then wonders why the team does not take more ownership.

## Visible problems

- Staff repeatedly return for approval.
- The website no longer reflects the real business.
- Services and priorities are difficult to explain consistently.
- Marketing and communication depend on his availability.
- Growth creates more decisions rather than more freedom.

## Private pain points

- He is exhausted by being continually needed.
- He feels guilty when he becomes the cause of delay.
- He suspects delegation will lower quality.
- He is proud of the organization and afraid of weakening what made it distinctive.
- He knows the current model is not sustainable.

## Internal contradiction

He says he needs delegation, but frequently corrects other people’s work because doing it himself feels faster and safer.

## What they are protecting

**Quality**

His deepest fear is not simply losing control. It is watching quality, relationships, and judgment decline when he releases control.

## What they tell other people

- It is easier if I just handle it.
- They are good people, but they do not see the whole picture.
- I need this to work without becoming generic.

## What they admit privately

> Everything still depends on me, and I do not know how to change that without lowering the standard.

## Desired movement

Make Frederick’s judgment a shared organizational asset rather than a private bottleneck.

## Desired feeling

**Confident release**

## Buying triggers

- Growth or hiring
- Persistent approval delays
- A new website or service restructure
- Founder exhaustion
- A team transition
- Repeated communication errors

## What they may have already tried

- Hiring a marketing or communications person
- Adding project-management tools
- Rewriting documents himself
- Creating scattered templates
- Delegating tasks without transferring decision logic
- Experimenting with AI tools

## What they resist

- Generic process consulting
- Long discovery without visible output
- Systems that add administrative work
- Technology positioned as a replacement for judgment

## Language they use

- Everything comes back to me.
- They need more context.
- I cannot be the only person who knows this.
- I need consistency without losing what makes us different.

## Language to avoid

- You need to let go.
- Your team just needs better tools.
- AI can replace this work.
- Scale at all costs.

## Primary public pathway

> Help my team move without everything coming through me.

## Working offer experience

A bounded capacity-building engagement that captures standards, clarifies communication, creates reusable resources, and improves decision and approval pathways.

## Emotional impact of the work

The work gives Frederick evidence that quality can survive outside his immediate involvement.

## Observable completion evidence

- Fewer decisions require founder approval.
- Core standards and messages are documented.
- Team members can prepare stronger first drafts.
- Website and service language reflect the current business.
- Approval and knowledge pathways are clearer.

## Likely objections

- Nobody understands the business the way I do.
- I do not have time to explain all of this.
- We already have tools.
- I cannot afford a long disruption.

## Decision authority and budget considerations

Frederick usually controls or strongly influences the budget. Commercial fit is strongest when the organization has existing revenue, staff, and a visible cost of continued founder dependence.

## Qualification signals

- Existing organization and revenue
- Staff or collaborators who can use the system
- Willingness to document judgment
- A specific bottleneck affecting growth or capacity
- Authority to change communication and workflow

## Disqualification signals

- Wants personal assistance without organizational change
- Expects unlimited access
- Will not allow staff participation
- Wants broad management consulting outside communication and workflow scope

## Relationship to AI

Curious but cautious. He wants AI to reduce repetition and improve preparation, but not to flatten voice, weaken judgment, or create more tools to manage.

## Relationship to control

High. Participation options and visible checkpoints reduce the fear that the process will take the business away from him.

## Relationship to visibility

Usually comfortable with organizational visibility but may prefer to remain less personally prominent.
