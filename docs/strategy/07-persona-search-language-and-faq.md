# Persona Search Language and FAQ Strategy

## Purpose

Translate the internal audience/persona system into search language, FAQ topics, animated-video topics, headings, and internal-link opportunities.

The target is not a list of agency keywords. The target is the language a capable owner, executive, communications lead, expert, or responsible AI adopter would plausibly use when trying to solve a real problem.

## Qualification rule

A proposed keyword/question must pass both tests:

1. At least one persona would plausibly search it.
2. It resolves a question the persona actually cares about.

If a phrase only describes what oobCREATIVE sells, it is supporting language, not automatically a target.

## Persona-to-search lens

### Frederick — Bottlenecked founder

Likely underlying searches:
- how do I stop being the bottleneck in my business
- what can AI take off my plate
- how can my team work without everything coming back to me
- what should I automate in my small business
- how do I use AI without creating more work
- can my team build this ourselves with AI
- do I need an AI consultant or can I do it myself

Protected value: quality and control.

### Jonathan — Mission-carrying executive

Likely underlying searches:
- how should our organization use AI responsibly
- what should stay human when using AI
- what information should we not put into AI
- how do we create AI guidelines for staff
- how much do leaders need to understand about AI
- how do we explain complex work more clearly

Protected value: mission, people, accountability, trust.

### Brendan — Overextended communications lead

Likely underlying searches:
- how can AI help with communications work
- what marketing tasks can AI automate
- how do I reduce repetitive communication work
- how do we create a better approval workflow
- why does AI make more work instead of less
- how do I organize company knowledge for AI

Protected value: professional agency and communication quality.

### Devon — Expert with unpackaged value

Likely underlying searches:
- why don't people understand what my business does
- how do I explain a complex service simply
- how do I make my website explain what I do
- can AI help organize my expertise without making it generic
- how do I turn my knowledge into a product or tool

Protected value: intellectual depth and accurate representation.

### Eli — Responsible AI adopter

Likely underlying searches:
- how should a small business start using AI
- which AI tools does my business actually need
- what can AI actually automate in a small business
- do I need an AI consultant
- what should I never give AI
- how do I use AI safely with customer information
- what should stay human when using AI
- how much AI do I need to understand as a business owner
- how do I create AI rules for employees

Protected value: responsibility, accuracy, privacy, trust.

### Dave — Rebuilding leader

Likely underlying searches:
- how do I know if I need a developer or consultant
- how do I fix a failed website project
- can AI replace a developer for a small project
- how do I avoid getting locked into a consultant or platform
- how do I know if an automation is worth fixing or rebuilding

Protected value: decision safety and trust.

### Doug — Quietly ambitious professional

Likely underlying searches:
- can I build an app with AI without being a developer
- how can AI help me launch a small business
- can I build my own website with AI
- how do I test a business idea cheaply
- what can I build with AI by myself

Protected value: possibility and credible forward movement.

## First ten animated FAQ / Answers priorities

These are ranked as content opportunities using three criteria: persona fit, usefulness/search plausibility, and emotional/visual potential.

### First five

1. **Do I need an AI consultant, IT, a developer, or can we do this ourselves?**
   - Personas: Frederick, Eli, Dave, Doug
   - Emotional tension: I do not want to spend money just because I do not understand the decision yet.
   - Supporting search language: AI consultant for small business; do I need an AI consultant; AI consultant vs developer; can I do AI myself

2. **What can AI actually take off my plate?**
   - Personas: Frederick, Brendan, Eli
   - Emotional tension: Some of this work should not still require me.
   - Supporting search language: what can AI automate in a small business; AI tasks for small business; how can AI save time at work

3. **Can we build this ourselves now, or do we still need a developer?**
   - Personas: Frederick, Doug, Eli, Dave
   - Emotional tension: Have we been assuming something is too expensive or technical that may now be possible?
   - Supporting search language: build app with AI without coding; do I need a developer; AI app development for small business

4. **How do I use AI without making more work for myself?**
   - Personas: Frederick, Brendan, Eli
   - Emotional tension: I wanted less friction, not six subscriptions and another system to manage.
   - Supporting search language: AI creating more work; simplify AI workflow; how to use AI efficiently in business

5. **What should I never hand over to AI?**
   - Personas: Eli, Jonathan, Devon
   - Emotional tension: I want the benefit without giving away judgment, trust, or responsibility that should remain human.
   - Supporting search language: what should not be automated with AI; what should stay human; risks of AI in business

### Next five

6. **Which AI tool do I actually need?**
   - Personas: Eli, Frederick, Brendan
   - Emotional tension: I do not want to become an expert in dozens of products just to choose one.
   - Supporting search language: best AI tools for small business; which AI tool should I use; ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini for business

7. **How much of AI do I actually need to understand myself?**
   - Personas: Frederick, Eli, Jonathan
   - Emotional tension: I do not need to build everything, but I need to understand enough to stay responsible and in control.
   - Supporting search language: AI for business owners; what should business leaders know about AI; learn AI for small business

8. **My team is already using AI. What do I need to know?**
   - Personas: Eli, Brendan, Jonathan, Frederick
   - Emotional tension: I do not want to shut experimentation down, but I am responsible for what happens.
   - Supporting search language: employee AI policy; staff using ChatGPT at work; AI guidelines for employees

9. **What information should I never put into AI?**
   - Personas: Eli, Jonathan, Brendan
   - Emotional tension: I do not want convenience to become a privacy or trust problem.
   - Supporting search language: what data is safe to put in ChatGPT; AI privacy for small business; customer data and AI

10. **Where should we start if we only want one useful AI win?**
    - Personas: all primary AI-state personas
    - Emotional tension: I do not want an AI transformation. Show me one thing worth doing.
    - Supporting search language: how to start using AI in small business; first AI use case; AI workflow ideas for small business

## Video structure

Each 30–75 second animation should use:

1. recognizable business moment
2. lightly absurd or humorous escalation where appropriate
3. misconception or tension
4. useful distinction
5. one practical next move
6. optional deeper Answer/review link

Humor targets the situation or tool proliferation, never the user's intelligence.

## SEO/content integration

For each question that becomes a page or substantial section, define:

- persona(s)
- search question/intent
- emotional opening
- concise answer
- deeper explanation
- related service/capability
- related verified proof
- related next question
- video transcript/caption text
- internal links in and out

## Search-volume caution

These are strategically qualified candidate topics, not claims of exact search volume or ranking difficulty. Before large-scale publishing, use current search-console, keyword-tool, and SERP evidence to prioritize exact phrasing and geography while preserving the persona fit test.
