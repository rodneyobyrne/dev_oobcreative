# Jonathan — The Mission-Carrying Executive Director

## One-sentence definition

A mission-driven leader carrying the operational, emotional, and public responsibility of work that affects real people.

## Stable identity

Executive director, nonprofit leader, program director, healthcare or human-services leader, or community initiative leader.

## Organizational context

Jonathan serves clients, families, funders, board members, partners, staff, and the public. Meaningful work is happening, but the organization struggles to explain it consistently without oversimplifying it.

## Emotional temperature

**Heavy responsibility**

## A moment from their day

Jonathan rewrites the same program description for a funder, a family, and a board member, knowing each audience needs a different doorway into the same mission.

## Visible problems

- Messaging has accumulated rather than being structured.
- Staff operate reactively.
- The website tries to serve everyone at once.
- Impact stories are inconsistent or difficult to gather.
- Administrative communication competes with direct service.

## Private pain points

- He feels responsible for people who may be harmed if the organization fails.
- He is anxious about funding, staff capacity, and public trust.
- He worries that simplification could misrepresent the work.
- He feels isolated as the person expected to remain hopeful.
- He encourages others to protect their energy while treating his own exhaustion as part of the job.

## Internal contradiction

He wants the mission communicated more simply, but fears that simplicity may diminish its seriousness, complexity, or humanity.

## What they are protecting

**The mission and the people it serves**

Jonathan is protecting access, dignity, continuity, and the belief that the work matters.

## What they tell other people

- We have to be careful.
- The people we serve cannot wait.
- There is more nuance than the website communicates.

## What they admit privately

> I am carrying the mission and constantly translating it for everyone else.

## Desired movement

Make the mission understandable and shareable across the organization without stripping away its human meaning.

## Desired feeling

**Shared responsibility**

## Buying triggers

- Funding or grant cycle
- Board expectations
- Program launch
- Leadership or staff transition
- Website redesign
- Public misunderstanding
- Staff burnout

## What they may have already tried

- Rewriting mission language internally
- Adding more information to the website
- Creating isolated campaigns
- Asking staff to collect stories without a system
- Hiring tactical vendors without shared strategy

## What they resist

- Corporate or manipulative marketing language
- Storytelling that exploits participants
- Technology that increases burden
- Oversimplification
- A process that consumes staff time without building capacity

## Language they use

- We need people to understand the work.
- Different audiences need different information.
- We cannot lose the human story.
- Our staff are already stretched.

## Language to avoid

- Leverage the mission.
- Turn beneficiaries into content.
- We just need a stronger funnel.
- Automate the relationship.

## Primary public pathway

> Help us explain the work clearly without losing its meaning.

- Website Strategy and Visual Refresh
- Ongoing Communications Support

## Working offer experience

A mission-communication engagement that aligns audience pathways, core language, impact storytelling, and practical staff resources.

## Emotional impact of the work

The work reduces the pressure on Jonathan to personally translate the mission in every room and helps the organization carry it collectively.

## Observable completion evidence

- Distinct message pathways for major audiences
- Reusable program and impact language
- Ethical story-gathering process
- Clearer website pathways
- Staff resources for consistent communication

## Likely objections

- Our work is too complex for a package.
- We cannot take staff away from service delivery.
- We have limited budget.
- We must be careful with people’s stories.

## Decision authority and budget considerations

Jonathan may control spending but often answers to a board, funder, or budget cycle. Qualification should include funding availability, decision access, and realistic staff participation.

## Qualification signals

- Established funding or committed project budget
- Leadership and board alignment
- Genuine need across multiple audiences
- Willingness to establish ethical story and review standards

## Disqualification signals

- Philosophical fit without purchasing capacity
- No decision authority or sponsor
- Expects extensive discounted work because the mission is important
- Wants emotionally manipulative storytelling

## Relationship to AI

Interested in practical relief for lean teams but cautious about privacy, dignity, accuracy, and the risk of making human services feel impersonal.

## Relationship to control

Shared and accountable. Jonathan needs confidence that the process respects organizational governance and the people represented.

## Relationship to visibility

Often visible as a leader, but the goal is to make the organization and mission less dependent on his personal presence.

## Visual-generation notes

These notes describe emotional atmosphere rather than a fixed demographic identity.

- Weight and responsibility without portraying defeat
- Community, program, or organizational context
- Multiple audiences suggested in the environment
- Human warmth with restrained seriousness
- Avoid savior imagery or sentimental nonprofit clichés

## Representative quote

> We are doing important work. I need people to understand it without turning the people we serve into a marketing device.
